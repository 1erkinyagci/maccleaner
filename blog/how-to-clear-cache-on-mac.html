How to Clear Cache on a Mac Safely

MacCleaner · 2026-07-16 · 6 min read

Short answer

User app caches live in ~/Library/Caches and are safe to delete: they're temporary files apps rebuild automatically. Quit the app first, delete the contents of its cache folder (not the folder itself), then empty the Trash. Leave /System caches alone — macOS manages them.

Where Mac caches live

  • ~/Library/Caches — per-user app caches. Safe.
  • /Library/Caches — system-wide app caches. Mostly safe, but be selective.
  • /System/Library/Caches — macOS internals. Leave alone.

Clearing them by hand

  1. Quit the apps you're clearing.
  2. In Finder press ⇧⌘G and enter ~/Library/Caches.
  3. Open a specific app's folder and delete its contents. Keep the folder itself.
  4. Empty the Trash — otherwise nothing is freed.
Deleting cache contents never deletes your documents, settings or logins. Those live in ~/Library/Application Support and ~/Library/Preferences — different folders entirely.

What it costs you

Almost nothing. The next launch may take a moment longer while the app rebuilds what it needs, and a browser may re-download some assets. That's the whole downside.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to delete ~/Library/Caches?

Yes. Delete the contents of app cache folders rather than the folders themselves, and quit the apps first.

Will clearing cache delete my passwords or settings?

No. Those live in Application Support, Preferences and the Keychain — not in Caches.

How often should I clear caches on a Mac?

Only when you need space or an app misbehaves. macOS doesn't need routine cache clearing.
