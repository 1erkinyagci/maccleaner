Clear Adobe Cache (Photoshop, Premiere, Lightroom) on a Mac

MacCleaner · 2026-08-04 · 5 min read

Short answer

Adobe apps build large caches: Premiere and After Effects media cache, Photoshop scratch and temp files, Lightroom previews and the Camera Raw cache — often tens of gigabytes. All are regenerated from your projects and originals, so they're safe to clear. Clean the Media Cache in Premiere's preferences and clear Lightroom previews and the Camera Raw cache.

Where Adobe hides space

  • Premiere / After Effects: Media Cache (Preferences → Media Cache) — often the single biggest.
  • Photoshop: scratch disk temp files and Camera Raw cache.
  • Lightroom: previews (.lrdata) and the Camera Raw cache.

Clear it safely

  • Premiere/AE: Preferences → Media Cache → Delete Unused (or clear all).
  • Lightroom: Preferences → Performance → Purge Cache; delete old preview data for catalogs you're not using.
  • Photoshop: Edit → Purge, and clear the Camera Raw cache in preferences.
Keep your project files, catalogs and original assets — the caches above rebuild from them.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to delete Adobe media cache?

Yes. The media cache speeds up playback and is rebuilt from your source media. Use Preferences → Media Cache → Delete Unused. Your projects and originals are untouched.

Where is the Adobe cache stored on a Mac?

Mostly under ~/Library/Application Support/Adobe and ~/Library/Caches/Adobe — media cache, previews and Camera Raw cache. Clear them from within each app's preferences.

How much space do Adobe apps use?

The caches alone often reach tens of gigabytes on an active creative Mac, all regenerable from your projects.
