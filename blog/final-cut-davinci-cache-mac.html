Final Cut & DaVinci Resolve Eating Your Mac's Disk (2026)

MacCleaner · 2026-08-04 · 6 min read

Short answer

Video editors are the heaviest disk users on a Mac. Final Cut Pro render files, optimized and proxy media, plus DaVinci Resolve's cache and gallery routinely total hundreds of gigabytes. Render files and caches are regenerated from your source footage, so they're safe to delete — Final Cut's Delete Generated Library Files and Resolve's Delete Render Cache reclaim the most.

Where the space goes

AppWhat piles upSafe to delete?
Final Cut ProRender files, optimized/proxy mediaYes — regenerated from source
DaVinci ResolveCacheClip, render cache, gallery stillsYes — rebuilt on playback/render
BothOld project libraries/backupsReview — keep active work

Reclaim it safely

  • Final Cut: File → Delete Generated Library Files → include render files and optimized/proxy media you don't need.
  • DaVinci Resolve: Playback menu → Delete Render Cache; and clear the CacheClip folder set in Project Settings.
Keep your source footage and final exports — those are irreplaceable. Everything above regenerates from them.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to delete Final Cut Pro render files?

Yes. Render and optimized/proxy files are generated from your source footage and rebuilt on demand. Use File → Delete Generated Library Files. Keep your originals and exports.

How do I clear DaVinci Resolve cache?

Playback → Delete Render Cache, and clear the CacheClip folder set in Project Settings → Master Settings. Both regenerate when you play back or render again.

How much space does video editing use on a Mac?

Render files, proxies and caches routinely total hundreds of gigabytes on an active editing Mac — nearly all of it regenerable from your source footage.
