256GB vs 512GB MacBook: How Much Storage Do You Need? (2026)

MacCleaner · 2026-08-04 · 6 min read

Short answer

For web, email, documents and light photos, a 256GB MacBook is enough if you offload media to iCloud or an external SSD. Choose 512GB (or more) if you shoot photos and video, keep large libraries, or develop — macOS and Apple Intelligence alone take 20–30GB before your files. And if you already have a full 256GB Mac, you can usually reclaim enough space instead of upgrading.

When 256GB is genuinely fine

If your life is browsing, email, documents, streaming and a modest photo library synced to iCloud, 256GB works — as long as you treat the drive as a working disk, not an archive. Keep media in iCloud with Optimise Storage or on an external SSD. Full playbook: living with a 256GB MacBook.

When to pay for 512GB or more

  • Photo/video shooters — libraries and exports fill 256GB fast.
  • Developers — Xcode, Docker, node_modules and AI models routinely need 50–150GB (see the developer guide).
  • Creative pros — Final Cut/Resolve and Adobe caches alone can top 100GB.
Remember the baseline: ~20–30GB is gone at setup for macOS and Apple Intelligence, before you add anything.

Frequently asked questions

Is 256GB enough for a MacBook in 2026?

For everyday use with media offloaded to iCloud or an external SSD, yes. For video, large photo libraries, development or creative work, 512GB or more is safer — macOS and Apple Intelligence take 20–30GB before your files.

Should I upgrade from 256GB to 512GB?

If you constantly hit the limit even after clearing rebuildable junk, yes. But most full 256GB Macs can reclaim enough space first — try that before paying for more storage.

How much of a 256GB Mac is usable?

Roughly 220–235GB after macOS, recovery and Apple Intelligence. Keep 10–15% free for macOS to run well.
