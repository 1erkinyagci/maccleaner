Mac Storage Not Updating After Deleting Files? Here's Why

MacCleaner · 2026-08-05 · 5 min read

Short answer

If your Mac's storage figure doesn't drop after deleting files, the usual causes are: the files are still in the Trash, macOS is holding purgeable snapshot space, or the Storage screen is showing a cached figure. Empty the Trash, give macOS a few minutes (or restart), and re-check. Disk Utility and df in Terminal show the truer numbers.

The three usual causes

  • Still in the Trash. Deleting is a move, not a delete — empty it or nothing is freed.
  • Purgeable space. macOS counts snapshots and caches as purgeable and frees them only when something needs the room.
  • Stale reporting. The Storage screen caches its breakdown; it can lag minutes behind reality.

How to see the real number

  • Disk Utility shows used/free including purgeable.
  • Terminal: df -h / for the filesystem view.
  • Close and reopen System Settings, or restart, to force the Storage screen to recalculate.
If free space swings on its own, that's normal APFS behaviour — macOS is releasing purgeable space on demand.

If it's genuinely not freeing

An app may still hold deleted files open (quit it or restart), or the files were on an external volume with its own .Trashes. And if the bulk is invisible, it's usually System Data — caches and developer junk. Frequently asked questions

Why is my Mac storage still full after deleting files?

Usually because the files are still in the Trash, macOS is holding purgeable snapshot space, or the Storage screen is showing a cached figure. Empty the Trash and re-check after a few minutes.

How do I force macOS to recalculate storage?

Close and reopen System Settings, or restart. Disk Utility and df -h in Terminal show the filesystem's real numbers immediately.

What is purgeable space on a Mac?

Snapshots and caches macOS can delete automatically when it needs room. It counts as used but is released on demand, which is why free space can change on its own.
