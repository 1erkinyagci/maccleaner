Freeing Up Space on macOS Tahoe: What Changed in 2026

MacCleaner · 2026-07-18 · 6 min read

Short answer

On macOS Tahoe (macOS 26), the storage fundamentals are the same as before — the biggest change in 2026 is the Apple Intelligence model footprint and the refreshed Settings UI. The built-in tools live under System Settings → General → Storage. The reliable safe wins are unchanged: app caches, developer artifacts, local snapshots that macOS manages, and the Trash.

What actually changed for storage

Tahoe brought a new look (the “Liquid Glass” redesign) and deeper Apple Intelligence, but under the hood your disk works the same way it did on Sequoia. The two things worth knowing in 2026:

  • Apple Intelligence now ships on-device models that occupy several gigabytes — see the dedicated guide.
  • The Settings layout moved things around, so the old “About → Storage” muscle memory is out of date.

Where the built-in tools are now

  1. Open System Settings → General → Storage.
  2. The bar at the top breaks down usage; hover segments to see categories.
  3. Under Recommendations you'll find Store in iCloud, Optimise Storage, and Empty Trash Automatically.

These are genuinely useful for photos and documents, but they don't touch caches, snapshots or developer files — which is usually where the real space is.

The safe wins that still work

None of the fundamentals changed, so the same honest checklist applies:

  • App caches in ~/Library/Caches.
  • Developer artifacts — DerivedData, node_modules, local AI models.
  • Purgeable snapshots — leave them; macOS thins them for you.
  • The Trash — empty it to commit the space.

The System Data question, again

Tahoe still lumps caches, logs, snapshots and AI models into System Data. If that bucket looks huge after upgrading, it's usually the new Apple Intelligence models plus the upgrade's own leftover caches — both normal, both explained in those guides.

Frequently asked questions

Is freeing up space different on macOS Tahoe?

The fundamentals are the same as Sequoia. The main 2026 differences are the Apple Intelligence model footprint and a rearranged Settings layout — the safe wins (caches, dev artifacts, Trash) are unchanged.

Where is the storage manager on macOS Tahoe?

System Settings → General → Storage. The Recommendations section holds Store in iCloud, Optimise Storage and Empty Trash Automatically.

Why did my Mac lose space after updating to Tahoe?

Usually the on-device Apple Intelligence models (several GB) plus temporary upgrade caches. The AI footprint is reclaimable by turning the feature off if you don't use it.
