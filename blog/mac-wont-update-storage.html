Mac Won't Update Because Storage Is Full? How to Fix It Fast

MacCleaner · 2026-08-04 · 5 min read

Short answer

A macOS update needs a large chunk of free space — often 20GB or more for the download plus installation. If your Mac won't update, free space fast with the safe wins (app caches, the Trash, developer artifacts), then retry. You rarely need to delete personal files: clearing rebuildable junk usually frees more than enough.

How much space an update needs

macOS downloads the installer and needs working room to apply it — commonly 20GB+ free, sometimes more for major upgrades. If you're short, the update stalls or won't start. The good news: you almost never need to touch personal files to get there.

Free enough space fast

  • Clear app caches — quick, rebuildable.
  • Empty the Trash — instant.
  • Developer artifacts — Xcode, node_modules, Docker — often tens of GB.
  • Still short? Find large files and offload media to iCloud or an SSD.

Working through the cleanup checklist in order gets you there fastest.

Retry the update

Once you've cleared 20–30GB, reopen System Settings → General → Software Update and try again. Frequently asked questions

How much free space does a Mac need to update?

Often 20GB or more for the download plus installation, and more for major upgrades. If you're below that, macOS won't install the update until you free space.

How do I free space so my Mac can update?

Clear app caches, empty the Trash, and remove developer artifacts — usually enough on its own. If still short, find large files and offload media to iCloud or an external SSD, then retry.

Why does my Mac say not enough space to update?

macOS needs working room to download and apply the installer. Clearing rebuildable junk (caches, Trash, build artifacts) almost always frees enough without deleting personal files.
