Why Apple Intelligence Is Using Gigabytes on Your Mac (2026)

MacCleaner · 2026-07-18 · 6 min read

Short answer

Apple Intelligence runs its models on-device, so macOS downloads several gigabytes of foundation models — commonly around 7 GB, more if you enable extra languages. macOS stores and manages them, so they show up inside “System Data” and can't be hand-deleted safely. If you don't use the feature, turning Apple Intelligence off in System Settings removes the models and reclaims the space cleanly.

Why an AI feature needs disk space at all

Unlike cloud assistants, Apple Intelligence does most of its work on your Mac. That's the privacy win — your requests don't all leave the device — but it means the models have to live locally. In 2026 that footprint is typically around 7 GB, and it grows when you add languages or turn on more features.

The space doesn't appear as a neat “Apple Intelligence” line. macOS files it under System Data, which is exactly why people notice their Mac quietly lost several gigabytes after updating.

Where it actually lives

The models sit in system-managed asset storage (under /System/Library and the private asset caches macOS controls). This is not a folder to go digging in — it's protected by System Integrity Protection, and deleting files there by hand can break the feature or the OS.

Rule from our safety guide: if a “cleaner” offers to delete Apple Intelligence model files directly, close it. Only macOS should manage them.

How to reclaim the space — the honest way

If you genuinely use Apple Intelligence, that 7 GB is doing its job; treat it like any other app you rely on. If you don't, reclaim it by turning the feature off, which tells macOS to remove the downloaded models:

  1. Open System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri.
  2. Turn Apple Intelligence off.
  3. macOS removes the on-device models over the following minutes; the space returns to “available”.

Turning it back on later simply re-downloads them. Removing unused languages (in the same pane, and under Keyboard) trims the footprint without disabling the feature entirely.

What not to do

  • Don't delete anything inside /System by hand to “remove the AI”.
  • Don't expect Optimise Storage or emptying the Trash to touch it — the models aren't user files.
  • Don't confuse this with local AI models you installed yourself (Ollama, LM Studio) — those are separate, much larger, and genuinely safe to delete.

Frequently asked questions

How much space does Apple Intelligence use on a Mac?

Typically around 7 GB for the base on-device models in 2026, and more if you enable additional languages or features. It's counted under System Data.

Can I delete Apple Intelligence to free up space?

Yes — turn it off in System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri and macOS removes the downloaded models. Don't try to delete the model files by hand; they're system-protected.

Will turning off Apple Intelligence delete my data?

No. It only removes the on-device model files. Your documents, photos and settings are untouched, and you can re-enable it anytime.
