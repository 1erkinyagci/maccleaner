The Complete Mac Storage Cleanup Checklist (2026)

MacCleaner · 2026-08-04 · 7 min read

Short answer

A complete Mac cleanup, in order of safest-and-biggest wins: clear app caches and logs, empty the Trash, clear developer artifacts (Xcode, node_modules, Docker, Homebrew, AI models), let macOS manage snapshots, then review Downloads, large files and Photos. Everything before “review” is rebuildable and safe. MacCleaner runs this exact order and pre-selects nothing personal.

Step 1 — the safe wins (do these first)

  • App caches & logs — instant, rebuildable.
  • Empty the Trash — nothing is freed until you do.
  • Developer artifacts — Xcode, node_modules, Docker, Homebrew, Python, AI models. Often the biggest win.

Step 2 — leave these to macOS

  • Local snapshots and purgeable space — macOS thins them automatically. Don't force it.
  • Anything inside /System — protected, and not worth the risk.

Step 3 — review the personal stuff

  • Large files, old Downloads, disk images and iOS backups.
  • Photos & video — offload to iCloud or an external SSD rather than delete.
The rule: everything in Step 1 is rebuildable and safe to clear aggressively. Step 3 needs a decision, not a trick — offload what you're keeping.

Frequently asked questions

What's the safest way to clean up a Mac?

Work in order of safety: clear rebuildable files first (caches, build artifacts, the Trash), leave snapshots to macOS, then review personal files. Never delete what you can't identify.

What should I clean on my Mac first?

App caches and the Trash — instant, safe, rebuildable. On a developer Mac, add Xcode, node_modules, Docker and Homebrew for the biggest wins.

How often should I clean up my Mac?

Only when space is tight or an app misbehaves. A quick monthly scan is plenty for most people; developers benefit from clearing build junk more often.
