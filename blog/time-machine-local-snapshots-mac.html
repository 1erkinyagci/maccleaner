How to Delete Time Machine Local Snapshots on a Mac

MacCleaner · 2026-08-04 · 5 min read

Short answer

Time Machine keeps hourly local snapshots on your internal drive; macOS reports them as “purgeable” and usually thins them automatically when it needs space. You can list them with tmutil listlocalsnapshots / and thin them with tmutil, but forcing deletion is rarely necessary — macOS frees them on demand. If your disk looks full from snapshots, that space is reclaimable when you actually need it.

What local snapshots are

Even between backups to an external drive, Time Machine saves hourly local snapshots on your internal disk so you can restore recent versions. They're counted as purgeable space — which is why your free space can look smaller than expected but jumps back when something needs the room.

How to view and thin them

  • List them: tmutil listlocalsnapshots /
  • Thin them (macOS chooses which): sudo tmutil thinlocalsnapshots / 10000000000 4
  • Or just let macOS handle it — it deletes snapshots automatically when free space runs low.
You usually shouldn't force snapshot deletion. macOS manages them well, and they're your safety net for recent changes. Only thin them if you genuinely need space right now and nothing else is available.

Where it fits in the bigger picture

Snapshots are one slice of System Data. If that bucket looks huge, snapshots plus caches are usually why. MacCleaner shows what's genuinely reclaimable versus what macOS is managing for you, so you clear the caches and build junk and leave the snapshots alone.

Frequently asked questions

Are local snapshots safe to delete?

They're your short-term restore safety net. macOS thins them on its own when space is low, so it's best to let it manage them rather than forcing deletion.

Why is Time Machine using space on my internal disk?
